# Reference minus invariant: what not to copy

A reference page is an **example, not a specification**. Its shape holds two
things at once — the pattern every screen of its kind shares, and that page's
own business — and copying it wholesale is exactly what produces a screen that
is almost right.

> **Reference minus invariant = that page's particulars** — the explicit list of
> what must not be carried across.

Nobody computes that by accident. It has to be done deliberately, before
writing, and said out loud.

## The procedure

1. **Assemble the family.** The screens this one is comparable with — see below
   for where they come from. **Three is the floor.** Two screens are a copy, not
   an agreement, and from an AST thirty pages sharing a convention and thirty
   repeating one old mistake are indistinguishable.
2. **Keep what repeats.** What most of the family does is the pattern.
3. **Name what appears once.** What only the reference does is that page's own
   business. Say it out loud before writing: *"the reference has a filter drawer;
   no other screen of this kind does, so it is not part of the pattern."*
4. **Write from the pattern, not from the page.**

## Where a family comes from, in order

1. **The route table.** The one place a project *states* which screens are
   registered beside one another. Stated beats inferred, always.
2. **The folders around the screen** — but a screen's own folder is not its
   family. A page with its grid, its dialog and its hooks beside it is **one
   screen**, not four, and the comparable screens are one level out. Where the
   router fixes the file name, they are one level up and one back down.
3. **A screen somebody named.** *"Use this page as the reference"* is a choice a
   person made, and frequency never enters it — so the risk this whole rule
   exists to prevent, enforcing the most-copied mistake, is not present at all.
   A named reference and a derived family are two different modes and they carry
   different weight.

## Two things that are not agreement

- **A majority is not unanimity, and which one is claimed matters.** *"Every
  screen of this kind writes this prop"* is a fact about the family. *"Most of
  them do"* is a different, weaker statement, and reporting the second as the
  first teaches people to stop reading.
- **A role answered differently on every screen is not a convention.** If they
  disagree, say so and ask. Picking the commonest enforces whichever was written
  first.
